# Odyssey ERP — Execution Plan

## Purpose

This plan turns the Odyssey ERP direction into an ordered sequence of product, business, marketing, and content work. It is a working plan and should be updated as evidence changes.

## Phase 1 — Foundation

**Objective:** make the product direction and operating model explicit.

- Confirm the target customer profile and the first operational problem to solve.
- Define the minimum vocabulary for organizations, users, workspaces, workflows, customers, and business records.
- Document the first end-to-end workflow rather than designing isolated features.
- Establish the distinction between planned, in progress, available, and validated work.
- Capture decisions, assumptions, owners, and open questions in the knowledge base.

**Exit signal:** the team can explain who Odyssey is for, which problem comes first, and what a successful first workflow looks like.

## Phase 2 — Product validation

**Objective:** test whether the proposed workflow creates meaningful value.

- Interview representative founders, operators, and team leads.
- Map their current process, tools, handoffs, delays, and reporting gaps.
- Prototype the smallest useful workflow.
- Test usability with realistic business scenarios.
- Record objections, unmet needs, and evidence in the repository.

**Validation questions:**

- Does the workflow remove a frequent source of friction?
- Can a new user understand what to do without extensive training?
- Does the workflow produce information that supports a real decision?
- Would the intended customer replace or reduce an existing workaround?

**Exit signal:** repeated user evidence supports the first workflow and its value proposition.

## Phase 3 — MVP delivery

**Objective:** deliver a narrow, reliable operating experience.

Prioritize:

1. Workspace and access foundation.
2. One complete operational workflow.
3. Ownership, status, approvals, and activity history.
4. A simple dashboard or summary view.
5. Data export and basic integration boundaries.
6. Product feedback and issue capture.

Defer broad module coverage until the first workflow is reliable, understandable, and repeatable.

**Exit signal:** a small group of real users can complete the target workflow and return to the product without guided support.

## Phase 4 — Commercial readiness

**Objective:** make the product easy to explain, demonstrate, and adopt.

- Turn validated outcomes into concise positioning.
- Create a workflow-based product demo.
- Prepare onboarding guidance and implementation expectations.
- Define a transparent feature-status vocabulary.
- Collect proof points without overstating product maturity.
- Establish a feedback loop between customers, product, and content.

**Exit signal:** a prospective customer can understand the problem, the Odyssey approach, the expected outcome, and the next step.

## Phase 5 — Expansion

**Objective:** expand capability only where customer evidence supports it.

Potential expansion areas:

- Additional operational workflows.
- Customer and commercial operations.
- Finance controls and reporting.
- Integrations and automation.
- Advanced permissions and governance.
- Role-specific dashboards and planning.

Every expansion should include:

- User problem and target role.
- Expected business outcome.
- Dependencies and operational risks.
- Status and owner.
- Validation or adoption evidence.
- Documentation and content implications.

## Operating metrics

Track a small set of useful signals:

- Time to complete the target workflow.
- Percentage of users completing the workflow without assistance.
- Repeat usage over a defined period.
- Number of dropped or delayed handoffs.
- Time required to answer key business questions.
- Activation and retention of the intended user group.
- Qualitative confidence and clarity reported by users.

Do not optimize for feature count. Optimize for completed work, trusted information, and improved decisions.

## Immediate next actions

1. Name the first target customer segment.
2. Choose one operational workflow for validation.
3. Write the current-state process from the customer’s perspective.
4. Define the smallest successful outcome for that workflow.
5. Identify three to five people who can test the workflow.
6. Create the first evidence log with interview notes and decisions.
7. Convert validated language into the first marketing and product messages.
