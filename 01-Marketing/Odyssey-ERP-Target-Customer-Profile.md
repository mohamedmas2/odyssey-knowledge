# Odyssey ERP — Target Customer Profile

## Why this document exists

Odyssey ERP is designed for businesses that need better decisions, not merely better bookkeeping. This profile defines the first customer group the company should understand, serve, and validate with before expanding the product.

It prevents Odyssey from trying to serve every business at once and gives product, marketing, sales, and content teams one shared definition of the customer.

## What Odyssey's first customer looks like

### Company profile

The initial target is a growing small or medium-sized business that:

- Has between 10 and 100 employees.
- Has multiple teams or operational handoffs.
- Is generating enough activity that spreadsheets and chat are becoming unreliable.
- Has an owner, general manager, or operations leader who still feels responsible for understanding the whole business.
- Is willing to improve its operating processes, not only purchase another isolated tool.

The company may operate in services, distribution, light commerce, or another sector with repeatable workflows. Industry-specific features should follow validated demand rather than define the first product.

### Primary buyer

The primary buyer is usually one of:

- Founder or business owner.
- General manager.
- Head of operations.
- Finance or administration leader with cross-functional responsibility.

This person cares about the health of the whole business and feels the cost of disconnected information most directly.

### Daily users

Daily users may include:

- Operations coordinators.
- Sales and customer-facing teams.
- Delivery or fulfillment teams.
- Finance and administration staff.
- Team leads responsible for approvals and follow-up.

The buyer needs business visibility; daily users need simple, clear execution.

## The customer's core problem

The customer does not primarily suffer from a lack of software. The deeper problem is that business information and work are scattered across systems that do not share context.

Typical symptoms include:

- The same information is entered in multiple places.
- Managers ask for updates because dashboards cannot be trusted.
- Tasks are handed from one team to another without clear ownership.
- Approvals happen in chat and are difficult to trace later.
- Reports explain what happened too late to guide the next decision.
- Growth creates more coordination work instead of more leverage.
- The owner becomes the integration layer between teams and tools.

The business may already use accounting software, CRM tools, spreadsheets, messaging apps, and custom processes. Odyssey's role is to create a clearer operating layer across the business, not to dismiss every existing tool.

## Why the customer chooses Odyssey

The customer chooses Odyssey when they want to:

- See what is happening across the business without chasing people for updates.
- Connect operational activity to business performance.
- Give teams clear ownership and repeatable workflows.
- Reduce duplicate work and manual reconciliation.
- Make faster decisions using information they trust.
- Build a stronger operating foundation before the next stage of growth.

The central value is clarity: clarity about work, ownership, performance, and next actions.

## How to recognize a strong-fit customer

A strong-fit customer usually has at least four of these characteristics:

1. Leadership is actively involved in improving operations.
2. Multiple teams depend on the same business information.
3. Current processes rely heavily on spreadsheets, chat, or manual coordination.
4. The company has experienced missed handoffs, delayed reporting, or duplicated work.
5. A decision-maker can sponsor process change.
6. The business has a repeatable workflow that can be mapped and improved.
7. The company measures growth but struggles to explain operational causes.

A weak-fit customer is one that:

- Only wants basic bookkeeping.
- Is not willing to change existing processes.
- Has no recurring workflow worth standardizing.
- Expects a fully customized enterprise system before validating a core use case.
- Cannot provide access to the people who perform the work.

## Example customer scenario

A 35-person services company manages leads, proposals, delivery, invoices, and follow-up across separate spreadsheets and messaging groups.

The owner receives weekly summaries but cannot confidently answer:

- Which opportunities are most likely to close?
- Which client commitments are at risk?
- Which invoices require action?
- Where is the delivery team overloaded?
- Which operational issue is affecting revenue?

The company does not necessarily need more reports. It needs connected workflows, clear ownership, and a reliable view of what requires attention.

Odyssey can begin by improving one workflow, such as moving a confirmed sale into delivery with owners, deadlines, approvals, and status visibility. The broader business operating system grows from validated workflows like this.

## How teams should use this profile

### Product

Use the profile to prioritize problems that improve business visibility and cross-team execution. Reject features that add isolated capability without improving a meaningful workflow or decision.

### Marketing

Lead with the customer's operational pain and desired business outcome. Explain how Odyssey creates clarity across work, information, and decisions.

### Sales

Qualify for business complexity, recurring workflows, leadership urgency, and willingness to improve processes. Do not sell Odyssey as accounting software.

### Content

Create practical content about operational clarity, connected workflows, disciplined growth, and decision-making. Use customer situations rather than generic ERP terminology.

### Research

During interviews, ask how work is done today, where information changes hands, what decisions are delayed, and which workaround the customer would gladly remove.

## Future improvements

This profile should become more precise as Odyssey gathers evidence. Future revisions should add:

- Validated industry segments.
- Company-size patterns linked to adoption.
- Buying triggers and common objections.
- Role-specific jobs to be done.
- Successful onboarding conditions.
- Measured outcomes from early customers.
- Segments that should be excluded or served later.

The profile should change because of evidence, not preference. Every major revision should reference the customer research or business decision that supports it.
